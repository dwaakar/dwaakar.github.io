title: "Hydroclimatic Changes in the Marsyangdi River Basin, Central Himalaya
  Under CMIP6 Scenarios: Implications for the Water–Energy–Food–Ecosystem Nexus"
authors: Diwakar Adhikari, Madhav Upadhaya, Nitesh Khadka, Deep Narayan Shah,
  Lal B. Thapa, Ram Kailash Prasad Yadav, Ramesh Raj Pant
year: "2026"
category: Research Article
doi: https://doi.org/10.1002/met.70240
abstract: Climate change is fundamentally altering hydroclimatic regimes across
  the Himalayas, with cascading impacts on water, energy, food, and ecosystems
  (WEFE). We applied the SWAT+ hydrological model in the Marsyangdi River Basin
  (MRB) of central Nepal, using bias-corrected CMIP6 projections under SSP2-4.5
  and SSP5-8.5 scenarios to study hydroclimatic regimes. We employed an envelope
  approach spanning warm-wet, warm-dry, cold-wet, and cold-dry states to capture
  plausible futures for mid-century (2041–2070) and end-century (2071–2100). By
  end-century, warming reaches +3.0°C (SSP2-4.5) and +5.0°C (SSP5-8.5), with
  precipitation increasing by 13% and 27%, respectively, driven by monsoon
  intensification. Annual discharge rises by > 30% and > 50%, yet snowfall
  declines by ~20% and ~35%, eroding the cryospheric buffer that sustains dry
  season flows. Pre-monsoon discharge decreases under both scenarios,
  exacerbating early-season water scarcity. Climate extremes intensify with
  extremely wet-day contributions increasing up to 85% under SSP2-4.5 and more
  than tripling under SSP5-8.5, daily rainfall intensity strengthens by 45%–55%,
  and consecutive dry days lengthen by 30%–87%, signaling concurrent flood and
  drought risks. Sectoral consequences are compounding with dry-season
  hydropower deficits reaching 44% under drier futures, while wheat irrigation
  demand nearly doubles by end-century under SSP5-8.5; paddy remains stable and
  maize rainfed. Simultaneously, declining dry-season flows and intensified
  runoff increase environmental-flow stress impacting aquatic connectivity, and
  elevate sediment-related risks in Annapurna Conservation Area headwaters. Our
  findings reveal a pronounced seasonal mismatch between water availability and
  cross-sectoral demand in the MRB, underscoring the urgent need for
  coordinated, climate-state-informed WEFE planning to enhance resilience under
  future conditions.
