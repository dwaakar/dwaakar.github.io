# **Background**

The Bagmati River is one of Nepal's most culturally, religiously, and ecologically significant river systems. Originating from Baghdwar in the Shivapuri Hills and flowing through the Kathmandu Valley, the river supports urban livelihoods, cultural heritage, biodiversity, and ecosystem services. However, rapid urbanization, population growth, untreated wastewater discharge, riverbank encroachment, solid waste accumulation, and hydrological alterations have severely degraded the river over the past few decades. These pressures have resulted in declining water quality, biodiversity loss, reduced environmental flows, and deterioration of ecosystem services that are essential for human well-being.

# **Project Objective**

This project evaluates the restoration trajectory of the Bagmati River from 2000 to 2026 through an integrated socio-ecological framework. Unlike conventional restoration assessments that focus solely on environmental indicators, this study investigates the interactions among ecological conditions, governance interventions, and social engagement. The aim is to understand why ecological recovery has remained limited despite substantial investments, policy reforms, infrastructure development, and public participation initiatives.

# **Methodology**

The assessment synthesizes evidence from scientific literature, government publications, development agency reports, policy documents, and community-based initiatives. Restoration efforts including the Bagmati Action Plan, the Bagmati River Basin Improvement Project (BRBIP), the establishment of the High-Powered Committee for Integrated Development of the Bagmati Civilization (HPCIDBC), wastewater management programs, riverfront development, the Dhap Dam project, and the Bagmati Cleanup Campaign were critically reviewed to evaluate their ecological, governance, and social outcomes.

# Key Findings

The study reveals that despite more than two decades of restoration efforts and significant financial investments, ecological recovery remains limited. Water quality in urban stretches continues to exhibit severe pollution characterized by low dissolved oxygen levels, high biochemical oxygen demand, microbial contamination, and declining aquatic biodiversity. Governance improvements have expanded institutional capacity and monitoring systems; however, fragmented responsibilities, weak policy implementation, and delayed infrastructure development continue to constrain restoration outcomes. Socially, the restoration process has benefited from widespread public participation through cleanup campaigns and awareness programs, yet challenges related to resettlement, environmental justice, and community trust remain significant barriers to long-term success.

The findings demonstrate that restoration outcomes are determined not by individual projects but by the interaction of ecological processes, governance effectiveness, and social legitimacy. Progress in one dimension cannot compensate for shortcomings in another. While infrastructure investments have increased, ecological gains have lagged behind due to persistent pollution sources, inadequate basin-scale coordination, and socio-political conflicts. The Bagmati case highlights the importance of viewing river restoration as a long-term socio-ecological transformation rather than a series of isolated engineering interventions.

Future restoration of the Bagmati River should prioritize integrated river basin management, strengthened inter-agency coordination, comprehensive wastewater treatment, environmental flow protection, riparian habitat restoration, and continuous ecological monitoring. Equally important is the inclusion of local communities through participatory decision-making, transparent governance, and socially equitable resettlement practices. By aligning ecological restoration with governance reforms and community engagement, the Bagmati River can move toward sustainable recovery and provide valuable lessons for urban river restoration initiatives across South Asia and other rapidly urbanizing regions.
